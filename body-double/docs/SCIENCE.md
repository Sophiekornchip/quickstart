# The science behind Sidequest

Sidequest's mechanics are each anchored to a specific, replicated finding.
This document maps feature → research, and notes where the evidence is strong
vs. promising-but-emerging.

## 1. Body doubling

**Feature:** the always-present companion who narrates presence ("I'm right
here on the sideline") and witnesses your work.

Body doubling — doing a task in the passive presence of another person — is
one of the most widely reported self-management strategies in the ADHD
community, and early research is catching up to the lived experience.
A 2024 exploratory study (Eagle, Baltaxe-Admony & Ringland, *"Proposing
Body Doubling as a Continuum of Space/Time and Mutuality"*, ASSETS/CHI work
on ADHD technology) found body doubling functions across in-person, virtual,
and even *parasocial* presence — the double doesn't need to help or even be
human; it needs to be *there* and occasionally acknowledge the worker. This is
why platforms like Focusmate and "study with me" streams work.

Mechanistically it draws on **social facilitation** (Zajonc, 1965): the
presence of others raises arousal and improves performance on simple/routine
tasks — exactly the boring maintenance tasks ADHD'ers stall on. For
under-aroused ADHD brains (see §4), that arousal bump is medicine.

**Design consequence:** the agent's chatter is mostly *presence*, not
instruction. It says "I'm here" far more often than "do this."

## 2. Micro-steps and task initiation

**Feature:** every quest is shaped into 3–6 steps, each startable in under a
minute; getting stuck triggers "shrink the step," never "try harder."

- **Implementation intentions** (Gollwitzer, 1999; meta-analysis Gollwitzer &
  Sheeran, 2006, d ≈ .65): concrete if-then/next-action plans dramatically
  increase goal attainment vs. abstract intentions. "Walk to the sink and
  look at it" is an implementation intention wearing a funny costume.
- **Shaping / successive approximation** (Skinner): reinforce tiny
  approximations of the target behavior rather than demanding the whole
  behavior. This is the dog-trainer half of Sidequest's DNA, applied with
  consent and kindness.
- ADHD's executive-function profile (Barkley, 1997) makes *initiation* the
  bottleneck, not ability. Steps sized "almost silly" get under the
  initiation threshold.

## 3. Contingent stimulation: the feed that pauses

**Feature:** the dopamine feed streams only while you work; go quiet past the
grace period and it pauses.

This is **contingent reinforcement** — stimulation yoked to the target
behavior — the core operating principle of operant conditioning (Skinner) and
of effective behavioral parent training for ADHD (attention and rewards
delivered contingent on target behavior; withdrawn otherwise). Premack's
principle (1959) formalizes the intuition: access to a high-probability
behavior (consuming your special interest) reinforces a low-probability
behavior (the dishes) when made contingent on it.

**Design consequence:** the pause is immediate, matter-of-fact, and reversible
the moment you re-engage. Contingency, not punishment.

## 4. Temptation bundling + optimal stimulation

**Feature:** special-interest audio drips during boring tasks.

- **Temptation bundling** (Milkman, Minson & Volpp, 2014, *Management
  Science*): restricting a "want" (audiobooks) to occur only during a
  "should" (gym) raised gym attendance ~50% in the flagship RCT. Sidequest
  bundles your special interest to your chores.
- **Optimal stimulation theory of ADHD** (Zentall & Zentall, 1983): ADHD
  behavior partly reflects under-arousal; added task-irrelevant stimulation
  can *improve* performance on monotonous tasks (cf. studies showing music or
  white noise improving ADHD task performance — Söderlund et al., 2007, the
  "moderate brain arousal" model). Boredom is not a moral failing; it's a
  stimulation deficit the feed fills.

## 5. Variable rewards, novelty, and the locked vault

**Feature:** loot rarity rolls, surprise mid-quest treats, held-back reward
drops, rotating personas, and no way to fast-forward to the prize.

- **Variable-ratio schedules** (Ferster & Skinner, *Schedules of
  Reinforcement*, 1957) produce the highest, most extinction-resistant
  response rates of any reinforcement schedule. Slot machines exploit this;
  Sidequest points the same engine at laundry.
- **Novelty and dopamine** (Bunzeck & Düzel, 2006, *Neuron*): the substantia
  nigra/VTA responds to stimulus novelty per se — novel stimuli energize the
  same midbrain dopamine system implicated in ADHD (which shows altered
  reward anticipation; Volkow et al., 2009). Hence the ever-changing
  personas: the *companion itself* is a novelty dispenser.
- **Reward prediction error** (Schultz, 1997): dopamine neurons fire to
  *unpredicted* rewards. A reward you can preview or skip to generates no
  prediction error — which is why the treat jar fills only on completion and
  cannot be preloaded. The lockout isn't cruelty; it's what keeps the reward
  neurologically real.
- **Delay discounting in ADHD** (Sonuga-Barke's delay-aversion model):
  ADHD'ers steeply discount delayed rewards. Sidequest shortens every delay —
  XP per step, surprise treats mid-quest — so no reward is ever more than
  minutes away.

## 6. Gamification, streaks, and precommitment

- Meta-analyses of gamification in behavior change show small-to-moderate
  positive effects, strongest when game elements map to real progress
  (Sailer & Homner, 2020; Johnson et al., 2016 for health apps).
- **Precommitment** (Ariely & Wertenbroch, 2002): people voluntarily bind
  their future selves, and binding works. Sidequest's "you can't skip to the
  reward" is a precommitment device you opt into once, at install.
- **Goal-gradient & streaks** (Hull, 1932; Kivetz et al., 2006): effort
  accelerates near goals; visible progress (step 3 of 5) and streaks exploit
  the gradient honestly.

## An honest note on limits

Body doubling and ADHD-specific gamified coaching are *emerging* evidence
areas — strong community validation and solid adjacent-mechanism RCTs
(temptation bundling, implementation intentions, contingency management), but
few direct large RCTs yet. Sidequest is a self-management aid, not a
treatment, and is designed to complement — never replace — clinical care.
The contingency design (feed pausing, locked rewards) is opt-in and always
escapable via "pause quest": supportive friction, not coercion.
